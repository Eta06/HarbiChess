# Own-terminal paired counterfactual pilot

This was a bounded CPU feasibility diagnostic, not training or a strength test. It tested whether paired action outcomes from the agent's own completed-game histories could provide sufficiently certain preference labels to justify a later learning ablation.

The predeclared sample was 16 lag-stratified full-history roots (lags 1, 2, 4, 8, 16), the frozen e8 policy's top two legal actions at each root, 64 common-uniform continuation seeds per action pair, and a 96-ply-per-arm cap. The roots, actions, and random uniforms were committed before branch outcomes. Both arms used the frozen policy, and common random numbers were mapped through each arm's own legal masked policy distribution. A cap was scored as unknown, with adversarial outcome interval [-1,+1]. No historical result, Stockfish label, optimizer update, model mutation, or strength game was used.

The run completed in 118.846 CPU seconds. Both arms completed in 420/1,024 pairs (41.0%), above the 25% feasibility floor, but none of the 16 roots had a positive simultaneous lower confidence bound (the frozen gate required at least eight). With alpha=.05 and 64 seeds per root, the Hoeffding radius was 1.114. Therefore the result is **NO_GO_NO_UPDATE**. The signal was too uncertain to justify applying preference updates under the predeclared gate.

Direct rule verification found only one exact one-ply terminal proof among selected top-two actions and no two-ply proof. A separate exhaustive scan of 134 own terminal-history prefixes at lags 1–2 found 343 one-ply proof actions and only 3 strict two-ply proofs across 2,705 legal actions; current e8 top-two choices included 50 one-ply proofs and zero two-ply proofs. This makes a proof-only preference rescue too sparse in this slice.

All full branch histories, including unknown/capped branches, and the frozen root/action/uniform selection are retained in `own-terminal-counterfactual-pilot-20261004T200900Z/`. The rule-proof scan receipt is `terminal-proof-coverage-20261004.json`. The method/ablation design and prior-art boundaries are in `terminal-counterfactual-next-method.md`. The paired rollout is a diagnostic, not an unbiased policy-gradient estimator: action-conditioned branch comparisons support only a local preference under the frozen continuation policy. A positive pilot would still have required a separate two-seed, broad held-out strength evaluation.
